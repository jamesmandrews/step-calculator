Steps in, calories out. No age field, because age isn't in the equation.

10,000 steps is a 1965 ad slogan

A Japanese pedometer was sold as the manpo-kei, "10,000-step meter." That's the whole origin. No study.

Calculators promising a step target "for your age and weight" divide a calorie number by a step cost. So does this one. You can just see the division.

What the burn depends on

Mass, distance, gradient. Nothing else.

The ACSM walking equation puts oxygen cost at VO₂ = 0.1·v + 1.8·v·g + 3.5 ml/kg/min, with v in m/min and g the grade. Drop the resting 3.5 for net cost, convert to distance, and speed cancels:

net kcal = (0.5 + 9g) × weight_kg × km

Flat ground, that's 0.5 × kg × km.

  • Pace is not in it. Brisk walking burns the same per kilometer as strolling. You finish sooner, that's all.
  • Incline is. A 5% grade nearly doubles the cost per meter. Hills beat hustle.
  • Age is not. Moving mass over distance is mechanics. Age changes how a walk feels, not the work done.

These are net calories

Net means above resting. Gross numbers are bigger and look better, but they count calories you'd burn on the sofa. Subtract those from your intake and you've counted them twice.

Why the loss slows

7,700 kcal ≈ 1 kg of fat. Holds for a few weeks. Then a lighter body burns less on the same walk, and appetite fills part of the gap.

The table shows both lines. At one year the honest one is about half the naive one.

No step count unlocks fat loss. More steps, more calories, near-linear. The threshold is invented.

Limits

Walking, 3–6 km/h, measured ground. Running costs more per meter, about 1 kcal/kg/km. Stride comes from height × 0.414, a population average. Measure yours if you can: stride error scales every number here.
